Here's the part that should mess with you a little.
Because everything about your daily life is telling you evolution is finished with you.
Central heating.
Soft food.
A world that no longer asks your jaw to fight for a single calorie.
It's easy to believe your body is a finished product.
It isn't.
Not even close.
You are still mid-experiment.
Rewind hundreds of thousands of years, to a version of you standing upright on an open, dangerous landscape for the very first time.
No mirrors.
No dentists.
No idea what a face was even supposed to look like.
Your skull is swelling to hold a brain bigger than any primate before it.
Your teeth and jaws are quietly shrinking, as fire and tools start doing the work your molars used to do.
Every one of those changes was happening for cold, practical survival reasons — walking farther, thinking sharper, eating smarter.
Nobody was designing a face.
Nobody was engineering a chin.
But shrinking jaws and a growing brain rearranged the front of your face in ways evolution never specifically planned for.
And nothing ever stepped in afterward to smooth out the shape it left behind.
There was no separate switch for "give this species a chin."
It simply fell out of everything else changing at once.
Evolution doesn't clean up after itself.
It doesn't circle back and fix the loose ends.
It just keeps building on top of whatever already worked.
Every trait stacked on top of the last one, layer after layer, compromise after compromise, until eventually you were standing here.
That's the tightrope biology walks — shaping you through a thousand accidents you never agreed to.
Then leaving the evidence sitting on the front of your skull for the rest of your life.
You didn't choose this feature.
Neither did anyone else.
Nobody signed off on this design.
It just is.
It just happened to you, quietly, over a span of time too vast to really picture.
And somewhere in that mess of trade-offs and half-finished redesigns, your chin got left standing.
Chin and all.
A monument to nothing in particular.
